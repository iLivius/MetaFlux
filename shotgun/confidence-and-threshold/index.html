    Choosing confidence and threshold¶

    Two Kraken2/Bracken settings affect how many false species calls reach the final table: the Kraken2 confidence score and the Bracken read threshold. In the benchmark on this page, changing confidence had little effect on accuracy. The threshold had the largest effect of any shotgun setting tested, and a fixed value suits only one sequencing depth: at Bracken's default of 10, accuracy fell the more deeply a sample was sequenced.

    • shotgun.kraken.confidence — leave it at 0.15.
    • shotgun.bracken.threshold — set it to auto (the default).

    This page is the benchmark behind that recommendation: what was tested, how, and what it did and did not show. If you only want the setting, skip to The recommendation.

    Effect on a mock community¶

    Kraken2 classifies a read to the taxon its k-mers best match, however thin that match is. Bracken then re-estimates abundance from those calls. Neither step, by default, asks how much evidence a taxon actually has before trusting it — Bracken's own floor (-t, ten reads) has been ten reads since its first release, at any sequencing depth.

    Species-level results on a mock community of known composition, the ZymoBIOMICS Gut Microbiome Standard (D6331), SRA run SRR38976715 (BioProject PRJNA1474435), classified with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626 at Kraken2 confidence 0.15, the v2.5.0 defaults:

    Species scored Precision Recall F1
    Bracken's default, -t 10 53 0.283 0.882 0.429
    MetaFlux default, threshold: auto (-t 265) 19 0.737 0.824 0.778

    The mock's Fusobacterium, listed by Zymo as F. nucleatum, is scored as F. animalis, so calls of F. nucleatum, F. polymorphum and F. vincentii count as false positives. Bracken also reports Homo sapiens at both thresholds: human DNA in the sample, not part of the standard, so it is left out of the scores. Benchmark data gives the reasons for both.

    Recall fell slightly, from 0.88 to 0.82: Salmonella enterica, with 135 Kraken2 reads, fell below the threshold. Precision rose from 0.28 to 0.74. At -t 10, 38 of the 53 species scored were not in the mock. Bracken reports a species only when Kraken2 gave it at least -t reads, so each of them had at least ten Kraken2 reads. Most of them (29 of the 38) are in the same genus as a member of the mock and share conserved regions with it; database cross-contamination is another source. The five false species left at -t 265 are all Fusobacterium species closely related to the mock's F. animalis.

    Kraken2 confidence — keep it at 0.15¶

    Raising the confidence score removes far fewer weakly supported species than the Bracken threshold does, because Kraken2 does not reject a read that falls short of the confidence threshold: it moves the call up the taxonomy tree until the score clears the bar, and only reports "unclassified" if even the root fails. Raising confidence therefore mostly moves reads to coarser ranks, which leaves more reads for Bracken to redistribute.

    The measured consequence: raising confidence lowers the read count of species-level calls, and a rare species can lose a large share of its few reads. On the same D6331 reads, Salmonella enterica, 0.01 % of the mock's DNA, had 135 Kraken2 reads at confidence 0.15 and 85 at 0.20 (PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626; 134 and 87 with k2_pluspf_20251015).

    Raising confidence and applying a read-count floor both reduce the same small read counts, so together they remove rare species that are present. Wright, Comeau & Langille (2023) swept confidence 0.00–1.00 on simulated data: 0.60 gave the highest mean F1, while 0.15 gave the lowest L1 distance, that is, the most accurate relative abundances. 0.15 is the source of MetaFlux's default, and nothing here changes it.

    Bracken threshold¶

    Why a fixed number is wrong¶

    The right floor depends on how many reads a sample has. A species with 200 reads is better supported in a sample of 2 million classified read pairs than in one of 15 million: auto sets -t 100 in the first, which keeps it, and -t 750 in the second, which removes it. A fixed threshold is therefore right for one depth only. In the first benchmark behind the default, with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20251015, one CAMI II marine sample (sample 0) was subsampled to 2, 5 and 10 million read pairs and also used at its full 16.6 million. At Bracken's default of 10, F1 was lower the deeper the sample was sequenced:

    classified pairs F1 at fixed -t 10
    1.8 M 0.740
    4.5 M 0.667
    9.0 M 0.612
    15.0 M 0.576

    With the community and all other settings unchanged, F1 fell from 0.740 to 0.576 (22 %) as depth increased. A depth-scaled threshold over the same range stayed at 0.82–0.83.

    The rule¶

    threshold = max(threshold_min, threshold_alpha × classified_read_pairs)

    Units matter: classified read pairs, not raw reads, not trimmed reads. Kraken2 in paired mode counts a pair as one unit, and the classified fraction depends on how well the database covers the community: with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626, 99.8 % of the D6331 mock's read pairs were classified, against 47–75 % for the four wastewater metagenomes of the shotgun test dataset, at similar depth (5.3 million read pairs entering Kraken2 for the mock, 3.5–4.7 million for the wastewater metagenomes). Raw input reads would therefore be the wrong denominator.

    shotgun:
  bracken:
    threshold: auto            # or a fixed integer
    threshold_alpha: 5.0e-05
    threshold_min: 10

    With auto, MetaFlux reads the classified-pair count from Kraken2's own report after it finishes — the count does not exist until then — and computes the threshold per sample. The decision is written to that sample's Bracken log; for the D6331 run it reads:

    [bracken] auto threshold: 5e-05 x 5292994 classified read pairs -> -t 265

    Where alpha comes from, and what is and is not new here¶

    -t is Bracken's own parameter, and Bracken does not scale it: it takes a flat integer and does nothing else with it. A cut-off that grows with depth is not a new idea. Benchmarks usually filter on relative abundance, a fixed share of the reads (for example Ye et al. 2019), and KrakenUniq found that the best unique-k-mer threshold rose roughly linearly with sequencing depth (Breitwieser et al. 2018).

    MetaFlux adds a measured constant and computes the threshold for each sample automatically:

    • a depth exponent of 0.967, fitted in the first benchmark on one CAMI II marine sample classified at four depths (1.8 to 15 million classified pairs, the series above), consistent with a threshold directly proportional to depth
    • a coefficient, alpha = 5×10⁻⁵, chosen in the first benchmark (PlusPF k2_pluspf_20251015) and re-fitted on all 41 CAMI II samples with k2_pluspf_20260626, where the median optimum was 6.0×10⁻⁵; on the D6331 mock the optimum is several times higher (next section)
    • the auto computation itself, run per sample inside the bracken rule

    None of this is standard terminology. "Alpha" is the name given here to the proportionality constant; if you cite this elsewhere, describe it as a depth-scaled Bracken threshold with a benchmark-fitted coefficient, not as a published Bracken feature.

    How firmly alpha is established¶

    Community (samples) Species per sample Optimal alpha
    ZymoBIOMICS D6331, run SRR38976715 17 2.3×10⁻⁴ – 3.5×10⁻⁴
    CAMI II strain-madness, samples 0–9 20 4.1×10⁻⁵ – 1.2×10⁻⁴
    CAMI II marine, samples 0–9 256–381 5.2×10⁻⁵ – 2.1×10⁻⁴
    CAMI II rhizosphere, samples 0–20 73–126 1.5×10⁻⁵ – 2.6×10⁻⁴

    The optimal alpha is the -t with the highest species F1, divided by the sample's classified read pairs. D6331: the run in Effect on a mock community (PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626, confidence 0.15), where F1 reaches 0.903 for any -t from 1,191 to 1,832. CAMI II rows: the range of the per-sample optima in the 41-sample re-fit with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626.

    Pooling the 41 CAMI II samples gives a median optimal alpha of 6.0×10⁻⁵, 1.2× the default. One constant works for these samples despite the spread because F1 changes little over a wide range of alpha: using 5×10⁻⁵ everywhere costs a mean of 0.023 F1 against tuning each sample to its own best (median cost 0.014). In the first benchmark, Bracken's default of 10 cost up to 0.34 F1.

    The D6331 mock is an exception: there the default costs 0.125 F1 against the optimum (0.778 against 0.903).

    Adjusting alpha for a known community

    A smaller threshold_alpha is a looser filter, a larger one a stricter filter. The best value differs between communities and between samples of one community (table above), and it does not follow the number of species: the optimum of the 17-species D6331 mock is 4.5 to 7 times the default, while the optima of the 20-species strain-madness samples range from 0.8 to 2.4 times it. The default is recommended for routine use.

    The recommendation¶

    shotgun:
  kraken:
    confidence: 0.15            # unchanged; do not raise it alongside the threshold
  bracken:
    threshold: auto
    threshold_alpha: 5.0e-05
    threshold_min: 10

    Worked examples: 2M classified pairs → -t ≈ 100 · 5M → ≈ 250 · 15M → ≈ 750.

    What this does — and does not — fix¶

    The threshold raises precision substantially, lowers recall slightly and changes abundance accuracy little.

    On the D6331 run, the L1 distance between the Bracken profile and the genomic-DNA composition in Zymo's manual (the sum of absolute differences, in percentage points) was 40.86 at both -t 10 and -t 265, and the Bray–Curtis dissimilarity between the two profiles was 0.0009. The threshold changes which taxa are named, not how much of each is reported. If your downstream work is ordination or differential abundance on already-good tables, expect little change. If it is species counts, richness, or presence/absence, expect a large one.

    It cannot rescue a database that does not cover your community. Only 165 of the 307 true species of the CAMI II rhizosphere (plant-associated) set, samples 0–20, are in PlusPF at all: 54 % over the whole set and 42 % per sample on average, the same in k2_pluspf_20251015 and k2_pluspf_20260626. No parameter setting recovers the rest, and species F1 stays near 0.3 at every setting tested. The gap lies in the reference database, and a database update alone should not be expected to close it.

    The benchmark behind these numbers¶

    Real data with known composition: the ZymoBIOMICS Gut Microbiome Standard (D6331), SRA run SRR38976715 (BioProject PRJNA1474435; see Benchmark data), 17 species after strain-collapse, with its Fusobacterium scored as F. animalis; classified with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20251015 and k2_pluspf_20260626.

    Simulated data with gold-standard truth: CAMI II marine samples 0–9, rhizosphere (plant-associated) samples 0–20 and strain-madness samples 0–9 (every strain-madness sample holds the same 20 species by design), 41 samples in all. All 41 were classified with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626; five of them were also classified with k2_pluspf_20251015 for the first choice of alpha.

    Real data with no known composition: the four wastewater metagenomes of the shotgun test dataset (BioProject PRJNA1477390, runs SRR39106521–SRR39106524). Without a truth table they give no accuracy figures; this page uses them for the classified fractions under The rule, and What it does on real data shows the k-mer evidence gate on them.

    Scorer check: the benchmark scorer was checked against OPAL 1.0.13, the CAMI assessment tool, on the Bracken species tables of the two runs with PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626 (Kraken2 confidence 0.15 and 0.20), which cover the 41 CAMI II samples and the D6331 mock. With its inputs prepared as the scorer prepares them, OPAL's species-level true positives, false positives and false negatives equal the scorer's in every sample. Run on the gold standards as distributed, OPAL differs only in the taxa those preparation rules touch: gold taxids that NCBI has merged since the CAMI release, placeholder names such as "uncultured bacterium", which the scorer does not score, and F. animalis, which the scorer counted as the mock's F. nucleatum in this check; the D6331 figures on this page score F. animalis as the true member instead. The first benchmark, the 41-sample re-fit behind the CAMI II rows of the alpha table and the k2_pluspf_20251015 runs were not part of this check.

    Comparator: MetaPhlAn 4, database mpa_vJan26_CHOCOPhlAnSGB_202605, on the same trimmed reads of the D6331 run. MetaPhlAn reported 14 of the 17 species and no false ones (F1 0.90). MetaFlux at its v2.5.0 defaults (PlusPF k2_pluspf_20260626) reported the same 14 species and five false ones, all of them Fusobacterium species related to the mock's F. animalis (F1 0.78). MetaFlux also reported Homo sapiens, from human DNA in the sample; it is not scored, and MetaPhlAn, whose database holds only microbial markers, cannot report it.
